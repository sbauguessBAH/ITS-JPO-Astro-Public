Road Weather Information Systems (RWIS) play a vital role in transportation operations by providing accurate, real time, and predictive information about weather and road conditions. These systems support transportation managers, road operators, and maintenance crews by enabling data driven decisions that enhance safety, reduce operational costs, and maintain efficient traffic flow, particularly during winter weather events.

The data provided by RWIS is used by transportation agencies at both national and local levels to support proactive planning and response. During winter weather, RWIS enables early mobilization of maintenance forces, more efficient use of materials and equipment, and improved coordination of operations. A component of RWIS is the Environmental Sensor Station (ESS), which serves as the physical infrastructure for collecting critical weather and road condition data. The ESS is equipped with a variety of sensors that monitor and record atmospheric, pavement, and water level conditions. These sensors provide real-time measurements of factors like air temperature, humidity, wind speed and direction, precipitation type and intensity, visibility, lightning activity, and storm cell movement. Atmospheric data collected by the ESS enables agencies to anticipate changing weather conditions that may impact road safety and traveler mobility.

In addition to atmospheric monitoring, RWIS collects detailed pavement data that is critical for winter maintenance decision making. Pavement sensors measure surface temperature, freezing point, surface condition such as wet, icy, or flooded states, chemical concentration from treatment applications, and subsurface conditions. This information helps determine when roads are likely to become hazardous and guides the appropriate timing and application of anti-icing and deicing treatments. RWIS also monitors water level data, including streams, rivers, lakes, and tidal levels near roadways. This capability is important for identifying flood risks, protecting transportation infrastructure, and ensuring safe travel in areas vulnerable to high water conditions.
