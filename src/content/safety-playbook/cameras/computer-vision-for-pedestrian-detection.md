Computer vision-based pedestrian detection is a technology that uses cameras, image processing, and artificial intelligence to detect, track, and analyze pedestrian movements in real time. It plays a critical role in improving traffic safety by alerting drivers to the presence of pedestrians, helping reduce collisions and supporting advanced driver-assistance systems such as pedestrian pre-collision warning and automatic braking.

This technology is also essential for autonomous and connected vehicles, enabling them to safely navigate complex urban environments where pedestrians, cyclists, and vehicles interact. By integrating camera-based detection with complementary technologies such as radar and lidar, autonomous systems gain accurate awareness of pedestrian locations and movements. Beyond transportation, computer vision supports smart surveillance by monitoring pedestrian activity in public spaces to enhance safety and situational awareness. It is also used to characterize pedestrian flows in areas such as intersections, sidewalks, and public squares, providing valuable data on pedestrian density, speed, and movement patterns. These insights help planners and agencies design safer, more efficient, and more pedestrian-friendly urban environments.

This technology uses advanced computer vision algorithms and cameras to detect pedestrians in real time. It enhances safety by providing timely and accurate detection of pedestrians, significantly reducing the risk of crashes in urban areas, near crosswalks, and in other high-traffic pedestrian zones.
