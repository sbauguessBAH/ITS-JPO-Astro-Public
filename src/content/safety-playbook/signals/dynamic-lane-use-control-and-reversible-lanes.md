Dynamic Lane Use Control is a proactive traffic operations approach designed to optimize roadway performance by dynamically managing how lanes are used in response to real-time traffic conditions. Using data from traffic sensors, cameras, and traffic management centers, the system can open or close lanes, shift lane directions, or designate special-use lanes to maximize facility throughput. This flexibility allows transportation agencies to make better use of existing roadway capacity without the need for physical expansion.

The strategy is especially effective in reducing congestion during peak travel periods, incidents, or planned special events by adjusting lane availability to match demand. Dynamic Lane Use Control also improves safety by providing advance warnings of lane closures or changes through overhead lane control signals and dynamic message signs. By clearly guiding drivers and managing traffic flow in real time, the system helps reduce sudden braking, confusion, and the risk of rear-end or secondary crashes, resulting in a safer and more efficient roadway environment. This technology uses electronic signage and lane control signals to dynamically manage lane usage based on real-time traffic conditions, incidents, and maintenance activities. It enhances safety by optimizing traffic flow, reducing congestion, minimizing the risk of collisions caused by sudden lane changes, and improving response times for emergency vehicles.

Reversible lanes are designed to adapt to changing traffic patterns by allowing lanes to operate in different directions at different times of the day or under special conditions. During peak traffic hours, these lanes are typically configured to provide additional capacity in the direction of heavier traffic, such as inbound travel during morning commutes and outbound travel during evening rush hours. This flexible use of existing roadway space helps improve traffic flow and reduce congestion without the need for roadway expansion.
